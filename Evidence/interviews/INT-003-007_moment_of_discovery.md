# INT-003-007 — The Moment You Knew

**Date:** March 15, 2026
**Subject:** Stephen Russell
**Interviewer:** Claude (ChappaquaPoison v3 editorial)
**Question:** The four poisoning discoveries are documented, but the physical experience of realizing what's happening to you is almost absent from the prose. Pick whichever discovery hit hardest — what did the moment actually feel like? Where were you, what were you holding, what happened in your body?

---

**Answer (verbatim):**

Discovery quickly turned to denial. The events that hit hardest were the two times Tara tried to have me committed. I hurt. I was exhausted. I was alone. it was horrific.

---

**Editorial notes:**
- I asked about discovery. He answered about commitment attempts. That's the correction. The poisoning discoveries are what the blog narrates. The commitment attempts are what actually broke him. The prose has the wrong climaxes.
- "Discovery quickly turned to denial" — this is why the discovery scenes feel thin. There was no moment of crystalline realization. The knowledge arrived and the mind rejected it. The prose should reflect that: not revelation, but a fact the body knew before the mind would accept it.
- "I hurt. I was exhausted. I was alone." — three sentences, no elaboration. Physical, then capacity, then isolation. This is the sparest Steve has been in any answer. The other answers had generosity, admiration, complexity. This one is stripped bare.
- "It was horrific" — the only adjective. He didn't reach for a metaphor or a frame. This is where language fails the narrator, and the prose should let it fail. Don't fill this in. Don't write what he can't say.
- The commitment attempts (Narrative of Madness, CORE_NARRATIVES #4) are currently narrated as legal/institutional events. Steve just told me they were the worst thing that happened to him — worse than being poisoned. The prose must carry that weight. The poisoning took his health. The commitment attempts took his reality — they tried to make the world agree he was the broken one.
- "Alone" — in every other answer, Steve is in relationship. Tara, Chappaqua, Petrella, Kelly, Brienne. Here he is alone. The commitment attempts severed him from every connection. That isolation is what made them worse than the poisoning itself.
- Relevant to: B08 (first commitment attempt), B15 (second commitment attempt — verify post mapping), and the Narrative of Madness thread across all posts. These posts need to be the emotional center of the blog, not the lab results.
