**Subject:** Stephen Russell
**Interviewer:** Claude (ChappaquaPoison v3 editorial)
**Question:** Brienne is the second narrator — her texts carry half the evidentiary weight of the story. But her actual relationship to the events is mostly implied. Is she someone who understood what was happening in real time, or did she piece it together later? What's her relationship to her sister now?

---

I don't know. Tara said something deliberately ominous the last time we spoke like Brienne was in some dire straight bc of her blog and opposition to the family. But it all felt like BS. Brie was hard to take with her Brown University, Chip on her shoulder, Brooklyn influencer thing, but I always like spending time with her and her husband. I thought she was courageous.

---

**Editorial notes:**
- "I don't know" — second time Steve opens with uncertainty (first was Kelly: "I don't know who she was"). The people closest to the evidence remain unresolved to him. This is the narrator's honest position and the prose must not pretend to know more.
- Tara's last communication framed Brienne as in danger for opposing the family — but Steve reads it as manipulation ("it all felt like BS"). This is evidence of Tara's method: weaponizing family concern as narrative control, even in the final conversation.
- "Hard to take" — Steve sees Brienne clearly. Brown University, chip on her shoulder, Brooklyn influencer. He doesn't idealize her. But he liked spending time with her and her husband, and he thought she was courageous. Same pattern: clear-eyed assessment followed by admiration that survives the assessment.
- "Courageous" — this is the word. Brienne's texts are evidence because she chose to put things in writing that her family would not want documented. The CAPTION_VOICE_GUIDE gives Brienne her own confessional register. Steve is confirming that register is earned — she was brave, not just talkative.
- The question of whether Brienne understood in real time or pieced it together later remains unanswered. Steve doesn't know. The posts should hold that ambiguity — the texts show what she said, not what she knew.
- "Brie" — familiar name. Despite the complications, this was family to him.
- Relevant to: B10 (Brooklyn — ambiguity rule in REVISION_STANDARDS), B22 (communication patterns), B25 (family dynamics), and every post using Brienne's texts as evidence. The caption voice should carry her courage without the narrator explaining it.
