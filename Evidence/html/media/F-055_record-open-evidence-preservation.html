The Evidence Preservation Challenge

In typical litigation, evidence can be:

  • Lost or misfiled in court systems
  • Destroyed in house fires, floods, or accidents
  • Intentionally suppressed or hidden
  • Degraded over time (documents fade, recordings lose quality)
  • Subjected to spoliation claims

The systematic archival and backup of all evidence in the Russell v. Walsh case ensures that none of these problems can occur. Even if the Westchester Family Court files were destroyed or lost, the complete record would survive intact in the independent archives.

Legal Significance of Complete Record

A complete, preserved record has several legal significances:

For Motion to Vacate: The complete record allows the motion to be decided on existing facts without need for additional discovery or evidence. The court can resolve it on the record.
For Appeal: If the motion is denied, the complete record preserved in the appeal brief ensures nothing is lost or mischaracterized on appeal.
For Federal Litigation: A complete §1983 case can be built entirely from the existing record, with exhaustion fully documented.
For Appellate Review: Higher courts reviewing the case have access to a complete, well-organized record that cannot be disputed as incomplete or selective.
