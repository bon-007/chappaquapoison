Court Document Facsimile

Example 1: DVRO Hearing — Q&A Testimony Format

The hearing took place in Department 514 of the San Francisco Superior Court. Judge Roger Chan presiding. The question came directly, and the answer came under oath.

Superior Court of California, County of San Francisco
Russell v. Walsh
Case No. FPT-18-377425 — DVRO Hearing, August 29, 2018
Tara Walsh
Respondent, examined by Petitioner's counsel
Q: Was he aware that you were giving him this medication?
A: No.
Q: How many times did you give him the Seroquel?
A: I put 100 milligrams of Seroquel in Petitioner's red wine.
LEGAL-DVRO-SEROQUEL-ADMISSION — Sworn testimony, DVRO hearing Sworn testimony, Russell v. Walsh, FPT-18-377425, San Francisco Superior Court. Source: PDF_DV_TRIAL_TRANSCRIPT.

She said it under oath. In a courtroom. Recorded by a court reporter. No ambiguity, no equivocation. One hundred milligrams of Seroquel in his red wine, and he did not know.

Example 2: Inquest Hearing — Narrative Testimony

Family Court, Westchester County, State of New York
In the Matter of Russell v. Walsh
Docket No. 154703 — Inquest Hearing, January 5, 2022
Tara Walsh
Petitioner, direct examination
Q: Can you describe what happened in March 2018?
A: He broke my phone, smashed it, tried to break my laptop, banged me around the kitchen against walls, and I had bruises all over my body.

No cross-examination was permitted. No evidence was admitted in rebuttal. The proceeding was an inquest — the respondent was in default and had no right to participate.

Email Facsimile

Example 1: Tara Walsh to Attorney — Death Warrant Email

On March 25, 2020, Tara Walsh sent an email to Max Di Fabio, her family law attorney. The language was not legal strategy. It was not a description of concerns. It was an accusation delivered as prophecy.

From: Tara Walsh
To: Max Di Fabio, Esq.
Date: March 25, 2020
Subject: Re: Custody arrangements

"Leaving Evie alone with Claudette, Linda or Steve would basically be a conscious decision of giving her up for adoption or signing her death warrant."

EMAIL_TARA_DEATH_WARRANT — Walsh to Di Fabio, March 2020 Email correspondence produced in discovery, Bates-stamped. Russell v. Walsh, CGC-18-570137.

Death warrant. For leaving a child with her grandmother and her father. The language manufactured emergency where none existed, creating a record of fear that could later be cited as proof of danger.

Design Notes

Court Facsimile: Times New Roman. Cream/parchment background (#faf8f4). Heavy top border on header. Q&A format with bold labels. Optional line numbers. Witness identification block. The reader instinctively knows this is a legal document.

Email Facsimile: System font header (From/To/Date/Subject). Georgia body. Warm paper background (#faf9f7). Attachment indicator with paperclip emoji. The reader instinctively knows this is an email.

Both formats: Amber exhibit ID in caption (consistent with existing embeds). Provenance text below in italic gray. Box shadow gives depth — documents sitting on the dark blog surface. Mobile-responsive with full-bleed at 768px.
